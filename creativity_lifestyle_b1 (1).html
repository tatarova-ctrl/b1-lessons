📖Reading text

Maya tells us about her creative life in Berlin. Target words and expressions are highlighted in yellow — watch out for them in the exercises.

"Creativity Isn't a Job — It's How I Live"

★ Maya, 26 · graphic designer, part-time DJ, full-time Berliner

People often ask me, "Maya, what do you actually do?" And honestly, I never know how to answer. By day, I'm a graphic designer at a small studio. By night, I'm a DJ. On weekends, I paint, or I go to the cinema, or I hunt for vintage clothes in flea markets. For me, creativity isn't something I do — it's the way I live my life.

I grew up in a creative household. My mother was a costume designer for the theatre, and my dad was a jazz pianist. When I was little, I remember our house was always full of music, books and half-finished projects. While my friends were watching cartoons, I was sketching in my notebook or making collages out of old magazines. My parents never told me to "get a real job." They just said, "Do what makes you come alive."

These days, my mornings usually start with a coffee and about twenty minutes of sketching — doodling, really. It sets the tone for the whole day. I genuinely believe you have to feed your creativity every single day, even in small ways. Sometimes it's listening to a new album on Spotify while I work. Sometimes it's watching a weird old film on a Sunday afternoon.

"Maya, you watch such strange films," my flatmate once told me. "That's the whole point," I said.

I'm really into indie cinema — low-budget films with strong soundtracks, unusual plots and characters that stay with you. Hollywood blockbusters are fine, but I prefer stories that push boundaries. The last film I fell in love with was a black-and-white Polish drama I saw at a tiny arthouse cinema. The cinematography was stunning — every single shot looked like a painting. I walked home that night completely blown away.

Music is the other side of my world. I've been DJing for about three years now. I got into it almost by accident. A friend was supposed to play at a small bar and he fell ill, so I stepped in. I was terrified. My hands were shaking. But when I saw people dancing to the first track I dropped, something clicked. Now I play at least twice a month. My sets mix old-school house, deep electronica and weird Japanese jazz. People either love it or hate it — which, honestly, is a good sign.

And then there's fashion. This one gets me in trouble sometimes, because I refuse to follow trends. I'd rather wear a weird second-hand jacket from 1978 than something everyone's wearing on Instagram. I've been into vintage since I was a teenager. There's something magical about wearing a piece of clothing that has a history, that someone else loved before you. I call it slow fashion — the opposite of fast fashion. It's kinder to the planet, and honestly, it looks way cooler.

The funny thing is, people sometimes say to me, "Must be nice to be so creative. I don't have a creative bone in my body." And I always disagree. I think everyone is creative — they just express it differently. Maybe you cook amazing food. Maybe you tell brilliant stories. Maybe you make the most beautiful Instagram stories in your friend group. That's creativity too.

If I could give one piece of advice, it would be this: don't wait for inspiration to find you. Chase it. Go to galleries. Listen to weird music. Watch films in languages you don't speak. Try on strange clothes. Get out of your comfort zone. Because at the end of the day, a creative life is a richer life. And you only get one.

📝Grammar 1: Past Simple vs Past Continuous

A quick reminder, then a mixed exercise using the creative-life vocabulary from this lesson.

✦ The two past tenses — quick guide

  • Past Simple → a completed action in the past (usually one thing, finished). I watched a film last night.
  • Past Continuous → an action in progress at a specific moment in the past. At 8 p.m. I was watching a film.
  • Together: use past continuous for the longer, background action, and past simple for the short action that interrupts it. I was painting when my friend called.
  • Key time words: past simple → yesterday, last week, in 2019, ago. Past continuous → while, as, at 5 p.m., all evening.
Past Simple: I worked / I didn't work / Did you work?
Past Continuous: I was working / I wasn't working / Were you working?
When + past simple, past simple. While + past continuous, past simple.

📝Grammar 2: Articles (a / an / the / —)

Articles are one of the trickiest things at B1! Let's review the main rules, then practise with art, music, cinema and fashion examples.

✦ When to use a / an / the / nothing (—)

  • a / an → something new or one of many. First time you mention it. I watched a film. / She is an artist.
  • the → something specific or already mentioned; also with unique things. The film was amazing. / The sun is shining.
  • no article (—) → with general plural or uncountable ideas. I love music. / Films are great.
  • Special cases:
    • ✔ the with cinema, theatre, radio: I went to the cinema.
    • ✔ no article with TV, school, work, home when talking about the activity: I watch TV. / I'm at home.
    • ✔ a / an for jobs: She's a DJ. / He's an artist.
    • ✔ the for musical instruments: He plays the piano.
    • ✔ no article for sports: She plays tennis.
