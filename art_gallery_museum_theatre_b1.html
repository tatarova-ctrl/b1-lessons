📖Reading

Read about Marcus — who went to a gallery expecting to be bored, and came out a changed man.

"I Thought I'd Hate It. I Didn't."

★ Marcus Petrov, 26 · IT Support Technician · Warsaw · 2025

Marcus had never been to an art gallery in his life. Not once. "I always thought it was for a certain type of person," he says, "the kind who uses words like perspective and chiaroscuro and actually knows what they mean." Then his girlfriend bought two tickets to a modern art exhibition at Warsaw's Centre for Contemporary Art, and he had absolutely no excuse to say no.

He arrived expecting to be bored. He was not bored. The first room stopped him dead — a giant installation of hanging mirrors and coloured light that transformed the space into something that felt like being inside a dream. "I just stood there," Marcus says. "I didn't know what it meant. I didn't need to know. It just looked incredible." His girlfriend — who studies art history — explained some of the ideas behind the work, but Marcus didn't find the explanations as interesting as the experience itself. "You should go to art and just feel it," he says. "You don't have to understand everything."

What surprised Marcus most was the social atmosphere. He had imagined silent rooms where you weren't allowed to speak. Instead, people were chatting, pointing at things, laughing, taking photos. A group of teenagers were debating loudly in front of a painting. A couple was having a quiet argument about whether something counted as art. "It felt more like a café than a church," Marcus says. "In a good way." They stayed for two and a half hours — twice as long as he had planned — and then went for coffee and talked about it for another hour.

Marcus now visits the gallery roughly once a month. He has bought a membership card. He still doesn't always understand what he's looking at. But he's stopped pretending that matters. "Could I explain what modern art is? No. Can I enjoy it? Absolutely."

🎯Collocations

Learn these natural word combinations used when talking about arts, culture, and leisure.

🎨 Art and culture

  • visit an exhibition = to go and see a display of art or objects. We visited a photography exhibition at the National Museum last weekend.
  • permanent / temporary collection = art always on display / shown for a limited time. The museum has a permanent collection of Impressionist paintings.
  • guided tour = a visit led by an expert who explains what you are seeing. The guided tour was fascinating — the guide knew everything about each painting.
  • contemporary art = art created in modern times, often experimental or challenging. She finds contemporary art more exciting than classical paintings.

🎭 Theatre and performance

  • book / buy tickets = to reserve seats in advance. You should book tickets early — the show sells out quickly.
  • live performance = something you watch happening in real time on stage. There's nothing quite like watching a live performance of a great play.
  • standing ovation = when the entire audience stands to clap enthusiastically. The cast received a standing ovation at the end of the show.
  • interval / intermission = the break in the middle of a show. Let's get a drink during the interval — there are twenty minutes.

😊 Reactions and opinions

  • be moved by = to feel strong emotions because of something. She was deeply moved by the final scene of the play.
  • make an impression = to have a strong effect on someone. The installation made a huge impression on Marcus.
  • thought-provoking = making you think seriously about ideas. The exhibition was thought-provoking — I kept thinking about it for days.
  • leave someone cold = to fail to produce any emotional reaction. Abstract painting completely leaves me cold — I just don't connect with it.

⚙️Modal Verbs for Opinions & Suggestions

Modal verbs are very common when discussing culture, giving advice, and expressing opinions. Study the rules, then complete all three parts.

⚙️ Modal Verbs — Key Meanings

  • should / ought to — advice or recommendation. You should visit the Tate Modern — it's amazing. / You ought to book tickets in advance.
  • must / have to — strong obligation or necessity. You must see this exhibition before it closes! / You have to book — it's fully booked.
  • don't have to / needn't — no obligation. You don't have to understand art to enjoy it. / You needn't dress formally for the gallery.
  • can / could — ability or possibility. You can take photos in most rooms. / We could go together on Saturday.
  • might / may — possibility (less certain). You might enjoy it more than you expect. / It may be a bit challenging at first.
  • can't / couldn't — impossibility. You can't speak loudly during the performance. / We couldn't get seats — it was sold out.

❔Question Forms & Indirect Questions

Asking questions correctly — and using indirect, polite question forms — is essential for social and cultural situations.

❔ Direct Questions — Key Rules

  • Yes/No questions: auxiliary + subject + verb. Do you enjoy going to galleries? / Have you ever been to the theatre?
  • Wh- questions: question word + auxiliary + subject + verb. What did you think of the exhibition? / How much does it cost?
  • Subject questions (who/what is the subject): no auxiliary needed. Who painted this? / What happened at the end?
  • ⚠️ What did you enjoy? ✅    What you enjoyed? ❌

❔ Indirect / Polite Questions

  • We use indirect questions to sound more polite. The word order becomes normal (no inversion).
  • Structures: Could you tell me...? / Do you know...? / I'd like to know... / I was wondering if/whether...
  • Direct: Where is the exit? → Indirect: Could you tell me where the exit is?
  • Direct: Is there a café here? → Indirect: Do you know if there is a café here?
  • ⚠️ After indirect opener, NO inversion: Could you tell me where is the toilet? ❌ → Could you tell me where the toilet is? ✅
