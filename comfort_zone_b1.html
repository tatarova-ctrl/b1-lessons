📖Reading text

Read about Lena. She tried something new and it changed her life. Words in gold are new and important.

"How One Class Changed My Life"

★ Lena, 29 · Lisbon

Three years ago, my life was very simple. I had the same job, the same friends, and the same plans every weekend. I wasn't sad — but I wasn't really happy either. I felt like I was always doing the same things. Then a friend asked me to try a theatre class with her. The class was in English. I was very scared.

My first thought was: "No way!" I am shy. My English is not perfect. The idea of acting in front of people was terrible. But for some reason, I said yes. And honestly, that one little decision changed my life.

The first class was hard. I felt very uncomfortable. I forgot my own name in one game! When I went home, I wanted to give up. I thought, "I am not going back." But I went back. The second class was a little bit easier. The third one was easier too. Slowly, I started to enjoy it.

This was my first real lesson about stepping out of your comfort zone: it doesn't get less scary, but you become braver. That is a big difference.

"Life starts where your comfort zone ends." — A famous quote

After the theatre class, other things felt more possible. I signed up for a public speaking workshop. I started a small art project. I went on a trip alone to Spain. None of these things were huge, but they all felt scary at the start. And every time I did one, the next one felt a little easier. I think I was building my confidence, like a muscle.

Of course, things didn't always go well. I made many mistakes. The art project was very slow at first. In Spain, I cried in a small restaurant because I was tired and I missed my mum. But here is the good news: the bad moments don't last. They become funny stories later. And every difficult moment taught me something.

What surprised me most is how my everyday life started to change. I began to speak more at work. I asked my boss for more money — and I got it! I tried new food that I always said "no" to. I think this is what people mean by personal growth. It is not really about doing huge things. It is about slowly changing what you believe is possible for you.

Stepping out of your comfort zone takes courage. Some days, I want to go back to the safe and easy life. Some days, I'm afraid of looking silly. But I have learned that this fear is a good sign. It means I am doing something new. As my theatre teacher always said: "If you don't feel a little uncomfortable, you are not learning."

If I could give one piece of advice to my younger self, it would be: stop waiting until you feel ready. You will never feel ready! Just start small. Pick one little scary thing and try it. The new you will say "thank you".

📝Grammar: Past Simple & Modal Verbs

Two B1 grammar areas you need: Past Simple (for telling stories) and modal verbs like can, should, must, have to.

🚀 Part 1 — Past Simple

  • Regular verbs: add -ed. I tried something new.
  • Irregular verbs (must learn): go → went, do → did, have → had, feel → felt, take → took, make → made
  • Negative: didn't + base form. I didn't give up.
  • Questions: Did + subject + base form? Did you try it?
Past simple: I tried. I went. I felt. I made.
Negative: I didn't try. I didn't go. (NOT "I didn't tried")

🚀 Part 2 — Modal verbs

  • can / can't = ability. I can swim. / I can't speak French.
  • should = advice. You should try a new sport.
  • shouldn't = negative advice. You shouldn't give up.
  • must / have to = necessity. You must / have to practise every day.
  • don't have to = it's not necessary. You don't have to be perfect.
  • would for hypothetical. I would try, but I'm scared.
All modals: modal + base form (NO -s, NO -ing, NO to). I can swim. (NOT "I can to swim")
