"Life Before Smartphones"

★ Nostalgia · 3-minute read

Last week, I tried to explain a Walkman to my 14-year-old nephew. He looked at me as if I came from another planet. "And you really liked it?" he asked. The honest answer is: yes! Life in the '90s was different. We didn't know any other way, and that was part of the fun.

The '90s feel like a long time ago now. There were no smartphones, no social media, and no Google. If you wanted to know something, you had to ask someone, look in a book, or just not find out. People used to argue for hours about which actor was in a film, because there was no easy way to check. Imagine that!

"In the '90s, being bored was a normal feeling. Today, it's almost impossible." — a writer

Music was different too. We recorded songs from the radio onto cassettes, hoping the DJ wouldn't speak over the start. We made mixtapes for our friends — and especially for the people we liked. A good mixtape took hours to make, and it was a special gift. Today, you just press "Add to Playlist" — and it's nice, but it's not the same.

The internet was new and very slow. People connected with dial-up, and you could hear that strange, screechy sound when the modem started. If your mum picked up the phone, the internet stopped! One photo could take five minutes to download. Email was magical. The idea of having internet on your phone was just science fiction.

Watching films was a different experience. You couldn't just press "play" on Netflix. You had to go to a video shop, walk around the shelves, and choose a VHS tape. Friday nights at the video shop were a special weekly ritual for many families. And if you missed your favourite TV show on Tuesday at 8 p.m., that was it — you missed it. No second chances.

Of course, the '90s weren't perfect. Mobile phones, when they came, were as big as bricks and very expensive. We used paper maps in the car and got lost a lot. We couldn't order food on an app. The world felt smaller and slower.

And yet, ask anyone who lived through that time, and they will tell you something was special. The pace of life was slower. People made plans and kept them, because once you left the house, nobody could text you "I'm 10 minutes late". You actually remembered phone numbers! You read books on the bus. You looked out of the window. There were whole afternoons when nobody could find you, and that was completely normal.

Maybe what we miss is not the technology — or the lack of it. Maybe it's the feeling. The feeling that you weren't always a little distracted, always checking your phone. Boredom wasn't a problem then; it was just part of life. And in those quiet moments, your imagination had time to grow.

So, were the '90s really "the good old days"? Honestly, no — and yes! They weren't perfect, but they had a quiet rhythm we have lost a little. As one person said: "We didn't have everything in the '90s. But maybe we had enough."

📼Then vs Now

For each topic, look at how things were then and how they are now. Which was better? Talk with your teacher!

🎵 Music
⏪ THEN
Cassettes & Walkmans

You recorded songs from the radio. You made mixtapes for friends. You couldn't easily skip a song. You carried only one cassette at a time.

▶ NOW
Spotify, Apple Music

Millions of songs in your pocket. The app picks songs for you. Music is everywhere — but maybe less special.

📺 Films & TV
⏪ THEN
Video shops & TV times

Friday night at the video shop. Choosing a VHS tape. If you missed a TV show, it was gone forever.

▶ NOW
Netflix & streaming

Watch anything, any time. The problem is choosing — sometimes you spend 30 minutes looking before watching!

📞 Talking to people
⏪ THEN
Home phones & letters

One phone for the whole family in the hallway. You learned phone numbers by heart. You wrote letters that took days.

▶ NOW
WhatsApp & video calls

You can call anyone, anywhere, instantly. Free. With video. Sometimes too easy — people expect a quick answer!

📷 Photos
⏪ THEN
Disposable cameras

24 photos per camera. You waited 3 days for them at the photo shop. No filters. Half were terrible — and that was OK!

▶ NOW
Smartphones

Unlimited photos. Filters. AI editing. Everyone is a photographer. We take 50 photos for one good one!

🎮 Playing & hobbies
⏪ THEN
Outside, with friends

Kids played outside until dark. Tamagotchis, yo-yos, board games, hide-and-seek. A "smart toy" was a Furby!

▶ NOW
Screens, screens, screens

Online games, YouTube, TikTok. Many kids don't play outside very much. Parents worry.

📝Grammar: Used to / Past Simple

Two B1 grammar areas perfect for this lesson: "used to" for past habits, and past simple for telling stories.

📼 Part 1 — "Used to" + verb

  • Use used to for things that happened often in the past, but not now.
  • Past habit: I used to play outside every day.
  • Past situation: There used to be a video shop here.
  • Negative: didn't use to (no "d" on "use"!) I didn't use to like coffee.
  • Question: Did you use to play with Tamagotchis?
used to + verb → I used to make mixtapes. (= I made them often in the past, but not now.)
Negative: didn't use to + verb (NOT "didn't used to")

📼 Part 2 — Past Simple

  • Use past simple for finished actions in the past.
  • Regular verbs: add -ed. I watched a film.
  • Irregular verbs (you must learn them):
  • go → went, have → had, make → made, be → was/were, see → saw, buy → bought, do → did, get → got
  • Negative: didn't + base verb. I didn't have a phone.
  • Question: Did you have a Walkman?
Past simple: I went / had / made / was / saw / bought / did / got
