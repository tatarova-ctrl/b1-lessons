📖Reading

Read about Jake — a 13-year-old from Manchester — and what he gets up to in his free time.

"I Don't Have Time to Be Bored"

★ Jake, 13 · Manchester, UK · Year 9 · Gaming, Football & YouTube

Ask Jake what he does after school and he'll laugh. "I don't have time to be bored," he says. His afternoons are packed with activities — and none of them look like what his parents did at his age. On Tuesdays and Thursdays, he trains with his local football team. On weekdays, he hangs out with his friends online — mostly playing Fortnite and Minecraft — for about two hours. On Fridays, he goes to a free coding club at school. And on weekends, he sometimes films short videos for his YouTube channel, which currently has 340 subscribers.

"Gaming isn't just sitting there doing nothing," Jake says firmly. "You're working as a team, communicating, making quick decisions. Last week we had a tournament and we came second." His parents aren't fully convinced, but Jake's school counsellor agrees with him: research shows that collaborative gaming develops real-world skills like problem-solving, communication, and resilience. The key is balance — not spending every waking hour with a controller in your hands.

Jake is also into making content. He started his YouTube channel six months ago after watching a tutorial on video editing. "I just film stuff — my football training, some gaming highlights, random things I think are funny." He does everything himself: filming, editing, adding music. He hasn't blown up yet — 340 subscribers is not exactly MrBeast territory — but he doesn't care. "I'm learning loads. And it's actually really hard to come up with ideas every week."

Not everything Jake does is digital, though. He reads graphic novels, cycles to school every day, and has recently picked up the guitar — badly, he admits, but enthusiastically. "My mum says I'll get into it eventually." According to surveys, 90% of teenagers Jake's age use YouTube daily. But the teens who stand out are the ones who don't just consume — they create, they play, they build things. Jake, it seems, is one of them.

📚Vocabulary

Learn these useful words about hobbies, activities, and free time.

hobby
An activity you do regularly for fun, not for work or school.
My main hobby is gaming — I play almost every evening.
content creator
Someone who makes videos, posts, or other media for an online audience.
Jake is a content creator with 340 subscribers on YouTube.
subscribers
People who follow and regularly watch a channel or account online.
He gained 100 new subscribers after his gaming video went viral.
tournament
A competition where people play against each other to find the best.
Their team came second in the online Fortnite tournament.
collaborative
Working together with other people as a team to achieve something.
Collaborative gaming teaches you to communicate and trust your teammates.
resilience
The ability to keep going and not give up when things are difficult.
Losing a match teaches you resilience — you try again next time.
balance
Having the right amount of different things — not too much of one thing.
Balance is important: gaming is great, but so is going outside.
tutorial
A video or guide that teaches you how to do something step by step.
He learned to edit videos by watching tutorials on YouTube.
highlights
The best or most exciting moments from a game or event.
He uploads his best gaming highlights to his YouTube channel.
subscriber count
The total number of people who follow a channel.
His subscriber count grew from 10 to 340 in just six months.

🎯Useful Word Combinations

These word pairs go together naturally in English. Learn them as chunks!

🎮 Gaming and technology

  • play online = to game using the internet with other players. We play online every Friday evening after school.
  • level up = to improve your skills or reach the next level. I finally levelled up after practising for two weeks.
  • go viral = when a video or post is shared by millions of people. His funny gaming clip went viral and got 2 million views.
  • upload a video = to put a video on a platform like YouTube. He uploads a new video every Saturday morning.

⚽ Sport and activity

  • join a team = to become a member of a sports group. He joined the school football team in Year 8.
  • train hard = to practise intensively and seriously. She trains hard three times a week to improve her skills.
  • take up a sport = to start doing a sport for the first time. He took up skateboarding last summer and loves it.
  • compete in a tournament = to take part in an organised competition. Their team competed in a regional tournament and won.

🎵 Creative hobbies

  • learn an instrument = to study how to play music. He's learning the guitar — he practises for 20 minutes a day.
  • edit a video = to cut, arrange, and improve recorded footage. She edits all her own videos using free software on her laptop.
  • come up with ideas = to think of new creative concepts. It's hard to come up with ideas for a new video every week.
  • grow an audience = to get more followers or viewers over time. He's slowly growing an audience for his gaming content.

⏱️Present Tenses

Practice the Present Simple and Present Continuous using hobbies and activities.

⏱️ Present Simple vs Present Continuous

  • Present Simple = things that happen regularly / always / never. Jake plays football every Tuesday. He trains three times a week.
  • Present Continuous = things happening right now or around this time. Jake is learning the guitar at the moment. He's getting better.
  • Key time words: Simple: always, usually, every day, never, sometimes. Continuous: now, at the moment, currently, these days, this week.

🔄Phrasal Verbs

These phrasal verbs appear in the reading. Learn what they mean, then complete the exercises.

🔄 Phrasal Verbs from the Reading

  • hang out = to spend relaxed time with friends. We hang out at the park after school on Wednesdays.
  • pick up = to start learning something new without formal lessons. He picked up the guitar by watching YouTube tutorials.
  • get into = to become interested in and enjoy something. I got into gaming when I was ten and never stopped.
  • come up with = to think of an idea or plan. It's hard to come up with a new video idea every week.
  • blow up = to become suddenly very famous or popular. His channel blew up after one video got 2 million views.
  • stand out = to be noticeably different or better than others. The teens who create content really stand out from the crowd.
  • keep up with = to stay at the same level as something that is changing. It's hard to keep up with all the new trends online.
