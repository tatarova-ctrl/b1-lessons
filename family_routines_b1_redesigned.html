★ Marina, 32 · Barcelona

My family and I have built a routine that makes us happy and keeps us in touch with what really matters. It hasn't always been easy to strike a balance between work and our hobbies, but we've managed to make time for the things we love.

My day kicks off at 6:30 AM. I get up early to do yoga for thirty minutes. It helps me set the tone for the day and gives me energy. My husband, David, prefers to sleep in on weekends, but during the week he has to stick to an early schedule because of work. Our teenage daughter, Sofia, loves to sleep till noon on Saturdays!

Breakfast is when we catch up with each other. We talk about our plans, our hobbies, and what's going on in our lives. My son enjoys taking up new activities — last month he picked up skateboarding, and now he's hanging out with his friends at the skate park every afternoon. Sofia is keen on photography. She spends hours fiddling with her camera, trying to capture the perfect shot. David is passionate about cooking. On weekends, he loves to whip up traditional Spanish dishes.

Our biggest challenge? We all have different schedules and interests. But instead of drifting apart, we make an effort to stay connected. We set aside Sunday dinner as sacred family time — no matter what, we all gather around the table. During these dinners, we bring up topics that matter to us, laugh about funny stories, and support each other's dreams.

Last year, Sofia took up tennis and asked us to watch her first match. We wouldn't dream of missing it! David has given up late-night television to devote more time to his cooking hobby. I've cut back on work stress by practicing meditation and yoga. My son is always buzzing with excitement about something new.

The secret? We understand that life is not about doing everything — it's about doing what matters. We make conscious choices about how we spend our time. We celebrate each other's interests and cheer each other on. And we know that when you make room for hobbies and family time, everything else falls into place. Family routines are not boring — they are the foundation of happiness.

② Vocabulary & Collocations

Master these essential collocations and expressions for talking about daily routines and hobbies:

to kick off to start "My day kicks off at 6 AM."
to get up early to wake up at an early hour "I get up early to exercise."
to set the tone to establish the feeling/mood "A good breakfast sets the tone for the day."
to sleep in to sleep longer than usual "I love to sleep in on weekends."
to stick to to follow/maintain something consistently "He sticks to his routine every day."
to catch up (with) to talk and share news "We catch up over breakfast."
to take up (a hobby) to start a new activity "I took up painting last year."
to pick up to start a skill/hobby "He picked up skateboarding recently."
to hang out to spend time together for fun "They hang out at the park daily."
to be keen on to like something very much "She's keen on photography."
to fiddle with to adjust/play with something "He's fiddling with his camera."
to be passionate about to have strong interest "He's passionate about cooking."
to whip up to prepare food quickly "He can whip up a meal in 20 minutes."
to drift apart to slowly lose connection "Families drift apart without making effort."
to make an effort to try hard to do something "We make an effort to stay connected."
to set aside to keep time for something special "We set aside Sunday for family."
no matter what regardless; in any case "No matter what, we have dinner together."
to bring up (a topic) to mention/introduce a subject "We bring up our plans at dinner."
to give up to stop doing something "He gave up TV for more hobbies."
to cheer on to encourage/support someone "We cheer each other on in hobbies."

Try to use: kick off · get up early · stick to · set the tone · sleep in · a typical day
◆ TASK 02 · YOUR HOBBIES & INTERESTS
What hobbies do you have? When did you take them up? How much time do you spend on them? Why are you keen on these activities?
Try to use: take up · picked up · keen on · passionate about · hang out · spend time on
◆ TASK 03 · YOUR FAMILY'S INTERESTS
Tell me about your family members' hobbies. What do they enjoy doing? How do you feel about their interests? Do you ever do these activities together?
Try to use: be keen on · passion for · take up · enjoy · support · cheer on
◆ TASK 04 · FAMILY TIME & BONDING
How do you spend quality time with your family? Do you have any special routines? How important is family time in your culture? How do you strike a balance between hobbies and family?
Try to use: strike a balance · set aside · catch up · make time · in touch · family bonding
◆ TASK 05 · A NEW HOBBY YOU WANT TO TRY
Is there a new hobby you want to take up? Why does it interest you? What challenges might you face? How would you start?
Try to use: take up · pick up · curious about · challenge myself · devote time to · make an effort
◆ TASK 06 · STAYING CONNECTED WITH LOVED ONES
How do you stay in touch with family and friends? Do you sometimes feel like you're drifting apart? How do you make an effort to stay close?
Try to use: in touch · drift apart · make an effort · catch up · no matter what · devote time
◆ TASK 07 · YOUR IDEAL WEEKEND
Describe your ideal weekend. How do you kick it off? What activities would you do? Would you include family? How is it different from your weekday routine?
Try to use: kick off · sleep in · set aside · hang out · ideal weekend · activities
◆ TASK 08 · ADVICE FOR SOMEONE
Your friend has no hobbies and feels bored. What advice would you give them? What hobbies might suit them? How should they start? Why is it important to have interests?
Try to use: take up · pick up · devote time · make time · worth trying · give it a go

⑧ Grammar Exercises

Master the essential grammar for talking confidently about daily routines and hobbies.

🚀 Grammar Rules You Need
PRESENT SIMPLE vs. PRESENT CONTINUOUS
Present Simple: For habits, routines, permanent facts
Example: "I wake up at 7 AM every day." / "She loves photography."

Present Continuous: For things happening RIGHT NOW
Example: "I am waking up right now." / "She is taking photos this moment."

Form: am/is/are + -ing
PREPOSITIONS (Time, Place & Manner)
In / On / At: in the morning, on Monday, at 6 PM
Place: in the house, on the table, at the park
Other: before breakfast, after school, during the week, throughout the day
ADJECTIVE + PREPOSITION
keen on (interested in): "She's keen on photography."
passionate about (very interested in): "He's passionate about cooking."
good at (skilled in): "She's good at sports."
interested in (like): "Are you interested in music?"
COMPARATIVES
Short adjectives: Add -er: faster, bigger, slower
Example: "My brother is faster than me."

Long adjectives: Use "more": more interesting, more difficult
Example: "Reading is more relaxing than gaming."
